The public content surfaces. Blog uses the browse chrome and the same "News and Articles" content the home feed rails; legal pages are the app's own set (Terms of Service · Privacy Policy · Refund Policy · Grievance Officer).

Decisions

  • One lead article, then carousels by topic. The newest piece gets a wide card, everything else rides rails — same rule as the rest of the set. No grid of identical cards.
  • An article caps at 760px and gets a table of contents at ≥1200. Reading measure matters more here than anywhere else in the product; a 1400px line of body text is unreadable no matter how much space is going spare.
  • Legal pages get a section nav instead of four separate screens reached from a list — same routes, same text, but a reader comparing Refund Policy against Terms does not have to go back each time.
  • Blog and legal keep the public header and footer, because these are the pages Google sends people to and a signed-out visitor must be able to get from them into the product.
